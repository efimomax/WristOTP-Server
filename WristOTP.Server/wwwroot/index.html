Security

Yes, it's actually safe

The short version: this server never sees your account name, your secret key, or your codes. It only ever holds a locked box it can't open, for at most 60 seconds.

🔒 In plain words

Your phone locks your account data in a box before it ever leaves the phone. The box is sent here, your watch picks it up, and the watch is the only other device with the key to open it.

The box disappears the instant your watch grabs it — or after one minute, whichever happens first. Nothing is written to disk. Nothing is backed up. There is no database.

🛠️ In technical words

  • Phone and watch each derive the same one-time key from your watch's device ID and a short sync code, via HKDF-SHA256 — the server never sees either input.
  • The payload is sealed with AES-256-GCM before it's uploaded. This server stores only ciphertext, an IV, and an auth tag — opaque bytes it cannot decrypt.
  • Storage is in-memory only (IMemoryCache), keyed by a derived lookup ID. TTL: 60 seconds, one-time read — a successful fetch deletes the entry immediately, TTL or not.
  • No logs, no persistence layer, no account database exists in this service at all — there is nothing to breach.

What the watch app does

  • Codes on your wristStandard 6-digit TOTP codes with a countdown ring — generated on the watch itself, no phone or internet needed.
  • FoldersGroup codes into folders (Work, Banking…). Deleting a folder never deletes the codes inside.
  • Sync from the phoneAdd accounts with a 6-digit one-time code — end-to-end encrypted, the relay forgets them within a minute.
  • Or type it inEnter an account manually right on the watch if you prefer.
  • App lockUnlock with your watch PIN on start — or every time you come back to the app.
  • Export by QRShow any account as a QR code to move it to another authenticator.
  • Made for a round screenCurved lists, scrolling with the crown, haptic feedback.
  • Your own relayPoint the app at a relay you run yourself — the server is open source.
